# User guide

For people using the dashboard, not for developers. The same guide is inside the app under **Guide**.

> Nothing here is investment advice. A profitable backtest does not mean a strategy will make money.
> Start in **paper mode**: it uses a simulated broker, so no real money can be lost.

## 1. What this platform does

You describe a **strategy** (for example "buy when the fast average crosses above the slow average").
The platform watches the market, and when the strategy signals, it:

```
signal  ->  risk check  ->  order  ->  broker  ->  position  ->  closed trade  ->  profit or loss
```

* The **risk check** can refuse any order. Refusals are shown in **Logs** with the reason.
* The **broker** is a simulated one in paper mode. Real brokers are only used when an administrator turns on several safety switches (section 9).
* **Profit and loss** always include brokerage, taxes and fees.

## 2. Your first 30 minutes

The **Guide** page has a checklist that ticks itself off as you go. The same steps in words:

1. **Check the top bar.** It should say `PAPER` and `LIVE TRADING: DISABLED`. If it does not, stop and ask the administrator.
2. **Risk page: review the limits.** Every order must pass them. Sensible starting values for a ₹5,00,000 account: daily loss ₹5,000, risk per trade 1%, at most 5 open positions.
3. **Strategies -> Create Strategy.**
   * Strategy: EMA Crossover is a good first choice.
   * Symbol and timeframe: try `RELIANCE` on `15 minutes` or slower.
   * Capital: the money this strategy may use. Keep it at or below the account capital.
   * Risk per trade: `1%`. Stop loss: `1%`. Target: `2%` or more.
   * Mode: `PAPER`.
   * Watch the **Good-practice review** card fill in as you type (section 5).
4. **Save, then Backtest.** Pick at least three months. Read the results (section 4).
5. **Start** the strategy. A confirmation dialog shows the mode and capital.
6. **Wait for a signal.** Signals appear only when a candle closes and the strategy's rule fires. On slow timeframes that can take hours. That is normal.
7. **Watch it work:** **Orders** (each order's history), **Positions** (open trades and unrealised profit), **Trades** (closed trades), **Dashboard** (totals).
8. **Practise the kill switch** once in paper mode (section 8).

## 4. Reading a backtest

| Number | Meaning | Rough guide |
|---|---|---|
| Net P&L | Profit after brokerage, taxes and slippage | Must be above zero |
| Profit factor | Money won divided by money lost | 1.3 or more is healthy, below 1 loses money |
| Max drawdown | Biggest fall from a peak | Under 10% is comfortable, 20% or more is hard to sit through |
| Total trades | Sample size | 30 is the minimum for any conclusion, 100 or more is better |
| Win rate | Share of winning trades | Not enough alone: a 40% win rate with big winners can beat 70% with small ones |
| Total charges | Costs paid | If it is more than half of the gross profit, the result is fragile |
| Sharpe ratio | Return relative to its ups and downs | Shown only with enough days of data |

Warnings to take seriously:

* **"Synthetic data"** banner: the prices were generated by the platform. Results say nothing about real markets. Connect a real data provider first.
* A backtest that **lost money after costs** is unlikely to win live.
* Results describe the **past**. Markets change.

## 5. The good-practice review

The **Good-practice review** card appears in the strategy builder, in the start dialog and in a strategy's detail view.
It gives a verdict and a list of checks, worst first.

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Recommended | Follows the common guidelines. |
| Caution | Usable, but some warnings are worth fixing first. |
| Not recommended | At least one serious issue. |

The review is **advice only**. It never blocks you, because you may have good reasons and paper mode is for experiments.
It does not predict profit.

What it checks:

| Check | Good | Warning | Serious |
|---|---|---|---|
| Risk per trade | 1% or less | Above 2% | Above 5% |
| Stop loss | Between 0.3% and 5% | Under 0.3% (noise) or over 5% (huge loss per share) | |
| Profit target | Reward at least 1.5 times the risk | No target, or reward smaller than risk | |
| Target vs costs | | Target under about 0.2% | |
| Timeframe | 15 minutes or slower | 1 minute (costs dominate) | |
| Strategy settings | Averages well apart, longer lookbacks | Very short EMA or breakout windows | Invalid settings |
| Backtest exists | Yes | Missing (paper) | Missing (sandbox or live) |
| Backtest is current | Settings unchanged since the run | Settings changed after the run | |
| Backtest data | Real market data | Synthetic data | |
| Backtest length | 90 days or more | Under 30 days | |
| Backtest trades | 100 or more | Under 30 | |
| Backtest result | Profit factor 1.3 or more | Profit factor under 1.3 | Lost money after costs |
| Drawdown | Under 10% | 10% to 20% | 20% or more |
| Costs | Under half of gross profit | Over half | |
| Real-money history | 30+ profitable paper trades | Paper record was a loss | Fewer than 30 paper trades |

## 6. Good habits

**Do**

* Stay in paper mode for weeks before thinking about real money.
* Risk 1% or less per trade and always keep a stop loss.
* Change **one** setting at a time, then backtest again.
* Trust a result only after 30 or more trades, ideally 100.
* Read **Logs** when something surprises you.
* Stop strategies you are not watching, especially short-selling ones near the market close.

**Do not**

* Trust backtests on synthetic data.
* Raise risk after a loss to "win it back".
* Run many strategies on the same symbol.
* Judge a strategy on a handful of trades.
* Share your login. Anyone with access can start and stop trading.

## 7. Understanding orders and positions

* An order moves through `CREATED -> SUBMITTED -> OPEN -> FILLED`. It can also end `REJECTED`, `CANCELLED` or `FAILED`.
* `PARTIALLY_FILLED` means only some of the quantity traded. The rest is still working.
* **Position size is calculated for you**: (capital x risk per trade) divided by (entry price minus stop price).
  With ₹5,00,000, 1% risk, entry ₹1,000 and stop ₹980, the size is 250 shares.
* A position closes when the stop or target is hit, when the strategy signals the opposite, when you close it, or when the kill switch is set to close positions.
* Stops and targets are checked every few seconds by the platform. They are not resting orders at a broker.

## 8. The kill switch

The red **KILL SWITCH** button is in the top bar on every page. After you confirm it:

* all strategies stop,
* new orders are blocked (orders that only reduce a position still work),
* waiting orders are cancelled,
* the system shows **HALTED**.

Open positions are **not** closed unless "close positions on kill switch" is enabled on the Risk page.
**Resume trading** lifts the halt, but strategies stay stopped until you start them again.

## 9. Going beyond paper trading

Paper mode is the default and never reaches a real broker, even if broker credentials are configured.

| Mode | What happens | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| Paper | Simulated broker | Nothing |
| Sandbox | Broker test environment, no real money | Sandbox credentials set by the administrator |
| Live | Real orders with real money | All four switches below |

Live trading needs an administrator to set `TRADING_MODE=LIVE`, `ENABLE_LIVE_TRADING=true`, the confirmation phrase,
and to arm a live broker account inside the app. Changing one setting does nothing. See the README.

Before anyone considers that, the review expects: 30 or more paper trades that were profitable after costs, a backtest on real data, and a drawdown you can live with.
Start with an amount you can afford to lose completely.

## 10. Adding teammates (administrators)

* The **first account created becomes the administrator**. After that, registration is closed.
* To let someone register, set `ALLOW_REGISTRATION=true` in `.env` and apply it with `docker compose up -d backend`. A plain `docker compose restart` does not re-read `.env`. Let them sign up, then set it back to `false` and run the same command again.
* **Everyone shares one workspace.** All users see the same strategies, orders, positions and trades, and any user can start, stop or kill trading. There are no per-user portfolios or roles yet. Only add people you trust with full control.
* There is no password reset screen yet. An administrator can reset one directly in the database.

## 11. Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Order rejected | A risk limit. Open **Logs** and look for `risk_check_failed`; it names the limit. |
| Strategy running but no signals | Signals fire only when a candle closes and the rule triggers. Slow timeframes are quiet. |
| Status shows HALTED | The kill switch is on. Use **Resume trading**, then start strategies again. |
| Strategy status ERROR | Its data or settings failed. The message is on the strategy row and in **Logs**. |
| Backtest says too few candles | The date range has no data for that timeframe. Widen the range. |
| Backtest is slow | A 1-minute test over months is millions of steps. Try 5 minutes or a shorter range. |
| "Cannot reach backend" | The backend is not running. Start it with `docker compose up -d`. |

## 12. Glossary

* **Signal**: the strategy's BUY, SELL or HOLD decision for the latest candle.
* **Stop loss**: the price at which a losing position is closed to limit the loss.
* **Target**: the price at which a winning position is closed to take profit.
* **Reward to risk**: target distance divided by stop distance.
* **Position sizing**: choosing the quantity so a stop-out loses only your chosen percentage.
* **Slippage**: getting a slightly worse price than expected.
* **Brokerage, STT, fees**: the costs of trading. They are included in every profit and loss figure.
* **Drawdown**: how far the account falls from its highest point.
* **Profit factor**: total winnings divided by total losses.
* **Paper, sandbox, live**: simulated, broker test environment, real money.
* **Kill switch**: emergency stop for all trading.
